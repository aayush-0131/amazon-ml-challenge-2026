## Source-backed technical comparison: BlackList and published Androids solution

The only **positively attributable finalist implementation reviewed as of this date** is [Androids' public repository](https://github.com/PardheevKrishna/amazon-ml-challenge-2026-entity-resolution). Its README claims public macro F0.5 **0.991300**, **12,293,019** final candidates and its own frozen implementation; these are **team-reported** figures, not an independent re-scoring and not a private final result.

| Dimension | BlackList EXP007, frozen | Androids, publicly disclosed | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Processing direction | S1-centric retrieval of S2/S3 | Incoming S2/S3 records retrieve S1 | Which direction is optimal under official hidden-test distribution |
| Blocking | Compact18 indexed tokens, union/budget limits | Five-channel inverted index: name/address tokens, character/phonetic n-grams | Exact recall tradeoff absent a shared dataset/split |
| Candidate ranking | Direct HGB features on candidates | CatBoost 76-feature ranker, keeps up to four S1 choices/incoming record | Whether reduction alone improved score |
| Pair model | 83-feature HistGradientBoostingClassifier | Two fine-tuned MiniLM/e5-family multilingual cross-encoders + features | Incremental benefit of encoders on a *common* holdout |
| Final decision | Source-specific fixed thresholds .965/.956 | Per-country LightGBM stackers, score/margin gating | Calibration comparability across different model scores |
| Error robustness | Numeric/name/address features; sampled negatives | Synthetic decoy clusters; explicit changed-number/legal-form modelling | Causal effect of engineered decoys |
| Country shift | General matcher, no France-specialized final route | Separate France normalization, pseudo-label adaptation/rules | France's contribution to public/private gap |
| Repair stages | No deployed rescue | India native-script and US/India address rescues | Whether these repairs increase unseen precision |
| Output candidates | 326,418,278 | 12,293,019 | Whether smaller output has greater or lesser recall |

**Important Androids caveats from its disclosure:** certain later France rules and rescue choices used public leaderboard feedback; its final submission did not undergo fully untouched end-to-end validation. This precludes treating the README as causal attribution or as a universal recipe. Its assumption of an incoming target's unique S1 owner must be checked on any future dataset.

**BlackList ground truth from frozen audit:** 4,869 false negative links on local EVALUATION, of which 1,677 were blocker misses and 3,192 were matcher/decision misses. Candidate count ≠ recall. [Details](POSTMORTEM_2026.md).

## What can be said about the other 49?

**They appear in the organizer's published sheet. Their algorithms are not established by that fact.** Generic repositories named 'amazon-ml-challenge-2026', same-name LinkedIn posts, team names like 'AdaBoost'/'Random Forest Rangers'/'BackPropagators', and historical leaderboard scores **do not establish team-specific model choices**. We did not attribute external solutions to these teams without an exact team ID, organizer citation, participant confirmation, or a repository clearly self-identifying as their challenge submission.

Thus it is *not scientifically possible yet* to compare all 50 teams' internal systems with each other. A numerical claim such as '42 used cross-encoders' would be fabricated. Maintain the provenance threshold below rather than decorating unknowns.

### Evidence-standard for later additions

Accept a technical attribution only with one of:
1. A public GitHub repository directly specifying the team **and** this challenge (ideally team ID), with actual code/method.
2. A publicly accessible presentation or engineering write-up identified as the team's own.
3. An organizer-linked team-method document.

For each accepted disclosure, add: source URL, publication date, team ID match, concrete components, metric/split, limitations, and whether code is reproducible. **Do not rely solely on college affiliation or a matching person's name**. Annotate self-reported scores as such.

## Questions for comparative research after the 7 October finale

- How did each publishing team trade blocker recall against candidate volume, *on the same definition* of recall?
- Was the fundamental matching unit S1-to-S2/S3 or reverse incoming-record linkage? Was target-owner exclusivity measured rather than assumed?
- Did methods operate per country and address unseen France without using hidden labels?
- What negative sampling and hard-decoy augmentation was used?
- Which neural/semantic models were actually fine-tuned and under what model-license constraints?
- Were final reported scores obtained on untouched validation, public feedback, or private leaderboard?
- What were the reproducibility, package, GPU and disk demands?

**Do not modify EXP007 or rerun TEST simply because another team's README looks more sophisticated.** The purpose is honest comparative learning after submission.
